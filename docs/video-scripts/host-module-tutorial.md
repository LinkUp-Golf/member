# Host Module — Video Tutorial Script

**Two videos from one script.** Chapters 1, 3, 4, 4.5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11 are the
host-facing tutorial (~9 min). Chapters 2, 5, 9 are marked **INTERNAL** — cut
those into a separate ops/SOP video for the LinkUp team (~5 min). Chapter 5 is
the GHL calendar setup, which is manual work and should never appear in the
host-facing cut.

Chapter 4.5 is the host-facing counterpart to chapter 5: the same calendar
work, described in one short beat so a host knows what they're waiting on, with
none of the how. Keep the two consistent — if chapter 5 changes, check 4.5 still
tells the truth.

**A host does not publish their own event.** They submit it; an admin publishes
it once the GHL calendar exists. Chapters 4, 4.5 and 5 all turn on that, and any
recording made before August 2026 says the opposite.

Everything in `[brackets]` is a screen direction. Everything else is narration.

## Chapter 1 — Applying to become a host (0:30 – 2:15)

[Screen: member app → **More** tab → tap **Become a Host**. Land on
`/more/host`. Header reads "Become a Host / Run your own rounds and earn
credits".]

> Hosting starts here — More, then Become a Host.

[Screen: the numbered "How it works" card. Pause on each step for about two
seconds as you read it.]

> The card at the top is the whole model in five steps.
>
> One — you tell us the private or semi-private club where you have a
> membership and want to host.
>
> Two — you give us the dates, the time, how many guests, and the cost per
> player, for each club. If you don't know the exact tee time yet, "morning" or
> "afternoon" is fine. And it really is dates, plural — one club, as many days
> as you want to run it.
>
> Three — we put those events on the LinkUp calendar.
>
> Four — members sign up and pay LinkUp. We transfer the money and the
> executive profile of each player to you before the event.
>
> Five — you submit proof that the event happened, with a group photo, and you
> earn LinkUp credit you can put toward golf.

[Screen: scroll to the application form.]

> The application itself is short — a name, and then the clubs you want to
> host at.

[Screen: type into **Host name**. Use something like "Jane Smith" or a brand
name.]

> Host name is how you'll be shown to members. It can be your own name or a
> brand you host under.

[Screen: the venue list under "Which venues do you want to host at?". Tick one.
It lifts out of the list into a card of its own.]

> Then pick your clubs. Tick one and watch what happens — it moves up out of
> the list into its own card. That card is where you tell us about the round
> you'd run there.
>
> The list underneath only ever shows what's left to pick, so you always know
> what you've chosen: it's whatever has a card.

[Screen: inside the card — set a date, then tap **+ Add another date** twice and
set two more.]

> Each card takes a date. If you'd run the same round on several days, tap Add
> another date and keep going — same club, same time, same price, three
> different dates. Each one becomes its own event on the calendar.
>
> That's why there's one card per club and not one per round: extra rounds at
> the same club are just extra dates.

[Screen: the tee time field, then **Available spots** and **Member guest
rate**, then the **Dinner included** checkbox.]

> Tee time is free text — "8:30 AM", "morning", "shotgun start", or leave it
> blank if it isn't settled. Then how many spots you're opening, what it costs
> you to bring a guest at your club, and whether dinner's included.

[Screen: tick a second venue. Its own card appears with its own fields.]

> Add another club and it gets its own card, with its own dates and its own
> price. Clubs don't have to match.

[Screen: tap **Add new venue**. A blank card appears with the name field
focused. Fill in the club name, the website, and the round beneath it.]

> If your club isn't on the list, Add new venue gives you a blank card. You
> fill in the club name and its website, and the round underneath, same as any
> other.
>
> For a club we don't have yet, all of that is required — the website included.
> We have to find the course and set it up before anyone can book it, and a
> name on its own can be three different courses in three different states.

[Screen: click into a field and leave it blank, showing the message appear
under that field.]

> Anything missing gets flagged on the field itself, as you go, so nothing is
> hiding further down the page when you hit submit.

[Screen: tap **Submit application**. The page reloads into the "Under review"
state with the yellow pill, listing the rounds that were submitted.]

> Submit, and your application is with our team. You'll see "Under review"
> here — along with the rounds you proposed, so you can check what you actually
> sent — and you'll get a notification as soon as it's been looked at.
>
> Nothing is created before you submit. If you name a club and then change your
> mind and close the page, that's the end of it.

## Chapter 4.5 — What happens on our side (7:00 – 7:30)

[Screen: stay on `/host/events` with the gold "Awaiting approval" card visible.
Keep this chapter short — it explains one wait, it isn't instruction.]

> So what are we doing with it while it says Awaiting approval?
>
> Behind every event there's a calendar on our side: we set it up for your
> round, put you on it as the host, and connect it to the booking and payment
> side so the money reaches you properly. That's manual work, and it has to be
> in place *before* members can book — otherwise someone reserves a spot that
> has nothing behind it.
>
> That's why the round waits for us rather than going straight out. It's on us,
> not on you — there's nothing more for you to do.
>
> When it's ready we publish it, and you get a notification the moment it goes
> live. That's your green light: members can see the round and reserve a spot.
>
> And if something about it doesn't look right to us — wrong club, a price that
> looks like a typo — we'll get in touch before it goes out, not after.

## Chapter 5 — INTERNAL: GHL calendar setup (7:00 – 9:00)

> **[INTERNAL CUT — do not include in the host-facing video. This chapter is
> manual work in Go High Level and it is our side of the process, not the
> host's.]**

[Screen: an admin phone or desktop showing the push notification "Hosted event
needs approval".]

> When a host submits an event, nothing is live yet — it's sitting in the queue
> waiting on us. Every admin gets a push notification: hosted event needs
> approval. That notification is your cue to start the GHL setup.

[Screen: admin app → `/admin/hosts` → **Events & Credits** tab → filter
**Awaiting approval**. Find the newly submitted event, with its amber panel:
"Not visible to members yet. Set up this event's GHL calendar and assign the
host first — publishing makes it bookable immediately."]

> Find it in Events and Credits, filtered to Awaiting approval. Check it's
> sensible first — right club, right date, a guest rate that isn't a typo. If it
> shouldn't go out at all, Take down cancels it with a reason the host sees.
> That's final; the host would have to create it again.

[Screen: switch to Go High Level. Open the calendars section.]

> If it's good, we set it up in GHL before publishing. This part is manual — the
> app doesn't do it for you, and it can't check you've done it. Publishing is you
> saying it's done.
>
> For each event you need to:
>
> One — create or pick the GHL calendar that this host's events run on.
>
> Two — assign the host as the calendar's owner or assigned user, so bookings
> against it land with the right person.
>
> Three — attach the event itself — the date, the tee time, the number of
> spots — to that calendar so it lines up with what the host submitted.
>
> Four — if the host has more than one event, repeat it per event. One host can
> have several calendars, and each event needs to point at the right one.

[Screen: walk through creating the calendar and assigning the host. Record this
at your own pace — it's the part people will pause and copy.]

> Take your time on this one. If the calendar and the host don't match up, the
> booking flows downstream go to the wrong place, and the host won't know why.

[Screen: back in the admin app on the same event card. Click **Publish to
members**. Toast: "Published — members can reserve a spot now." The badge flips
from gold "Awaiting approval" to green "Upcoming".]

> Last step, and only once the calendar is genuinely done: Publish to members.
>
> That's the moment the round appears on the member calendar and can be booked,
> and it's also what notifies the host their event is live. Nothing before this
> reaches a member, so there's no hurry — but nothing after it is reversible
> except a takedown, which releases anyone who's reserved.
>
> One per event. A host who submitted three dates has three cards to set up and
> three to publish.

> **Production note for whoever records this:** publishing is the confirmation
> now — the host gets a push notification from the app when you click it. There
> is no separate "setup complete" email to send by hand any more.

## Appendix A — Before you record

Set this up first so nothing is empty on screen:

1. A member account with **no** host row (for chapter 1). Chapter 1 now needs
   **at least three** bookable courses in the venue list — you tick two and
   still want visible options left underneath, since the list only shows what
   hasn't been picked.
2. A second member account that's **already an approved host**, with at least
   one venue granted (for chapters 3–4). Applying and approving live on camera
   works too, but it makes chapter 2 hard to cut out cleanly.
3. An admin account (chapters 2, 5, 9).
4. A third member account to do the reserving in chapter 6.
5. At least one **past-dated** hosted event on the host account, already
   **published** (`upcoming`), so the **Upload proof** button is visible in
   chapter 8 without waiting. A pending event never gets that button — it was
   never live, so there is nothing to claim credit for.
6. A group photo ready to upload — JPG/PNG/WebP, under 10 MB.
7. One event already in **Credit approval** for chapter 9.
8. Push notifications enabled on the host device, so the "someone joined" and
   admin notifications actually fire on camera.
9. A **pending** application carrying two or three proposed rounds, for
   chapter 2 — the round checkboxes are the new part of that drawer and an
   application without them shows nothing to demonstrate.
10. The four August 2026 database updates applied to whatever environment you
    record on (`npm run db:push`). Without them the application form fails on
    submit and chapter 1 can't be recorded at all, and events won't hold in
    Awaiting approval the way chapters 4.5 and 5 describe.
11. One event **awaiting approval** on the host account, so chapter 4.5 has a
    gold badge to point at without waiting for a submit to land.

| --- | --- |

| Members pay your guest rate plus the $10 LinkUp fee | Members pay $X |

## Appendix C — Accuracy notes for the narrator

- **The credit amount.** The "Become a Host" page pitches "$150 credit per
  event". What the app actually awards is the host's **member guest rate**, and
  an admin can adjust that figure before approving. If the guest rate is $150
  the two agree. Narrate it as *"your credit for this event is your guest
  rate"* — which is what the publish screen says — and you'll never contradict
  what's on screen.
- **Hosts submit; we publish.** A host-created event lands in **Awaiting
  approval** and no member can see it — not in browse, not by link. An admin
  publishes it, and that is also when the GHL calendar behind it gets built.
  Never say a host publishes their own event, and never call the wait optional.
  *(This changed in August 2026 — earlier recordings of chapters 4, 4.5 and 5
  say events go live on submit and are wrong.)*
- **Chapter 4.5 is the gate, and that's the point.** The manual calendar setup
  it describes is why the event waits: publishing before the calendar exists
  would let a member book a round with nothing behind it. Narrate it as "here's
  what we're doing before it goes out", and be straight that the host waits on
  us. The reassurance is that there's nothing more for *them* to do.
- **Nothing in the app checks the calendar exists.** Publish is an admin saying
  "the GHL setup is done". It's the only thing standing between a submitted
  event and a bookable one, so don't script it as a formality.
- **An approval creates events, it doesn't publish them.** Rounds left ticked on
  an application become events the moment an admin approves it — as
  `pending_approval`, like any other. So a brand-new host can have events queued
  before they've ever opened the host dashboard, and each still needs its GHL
  calendar and its own Publish click.
- **Applications no longer have a description.** The field was removed from the
  form. Older applications still show theirs in the review drawer; new ones
  won't have one.
- **A club a host proposes is created when they submit**, as a Pending course —
  not when they type its name. There is no longer a "Request venue" button, and
  nothing is created by a form that gets abandoned.
- **New clubs need a website.** On both the application and the event form, a
  club that isn't on LinkUp requires its name *and* its website. Don't script
  the website as optional.
- **Multiple dates are a create-time feature.** "Add another date" appears when
  submitting a new event and on the application form. It is not in the edit
  drawer — editing changes the one event's date. Each date is a separate event,
  which also means a separate calendar setup and a separate Publish.
- **Publishing is the host's confirmation.** There is no longer a "setup
  complete" email to send by hand — the app pushes "Your event is live" when an
  admin publishes. Push notifications cover every other transition too
  (application decided, event needs approval, someone reserved, credit approved
  or rejected, event taken down).
- **Venues are a permission, not a preference.** An approved host can only
  create events at the clubs granted on their application. Hosts approved
  before venues existed are unrestricted — if you're demoing on an old test
  account the course dropdown will show every course, which isn't what a new
  host sees.
- **Proof timing.** The Upload proof button appears when the event date has
  arrived — not the day after. It also stays available while an event sits in
  Credit approval, as "Replace proof".
