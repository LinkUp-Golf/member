LinkUp Golf · Design proposal

Open-Ended Curated Introductions

Replace the Curated Introductions checklists with written descriptions. AI reads them and suggests matches, and an admin approves every introduction before it is sent.

Draft for review · 24 Nov 2026 Not built yet

Why change it

The survey asks members to pick an industry and a role from fixed lists. No list short enough to use can cover every business: an HVAC company owner, a family office or an orthodontist has no accurate choice today. The current matcher also can't tell that "CPA" and "accountant" mean the same thing.

Members would instead describe themselves, and the people they'd like to meet, in their own words. Anything a member writes can count toward a match. Anything nobody mentions is left out.

Scope

Version 1 introduces members to other members of LinkUp Golf, only within their own community. Today every member is in San Diego.

Future paid versions may add either or both of these:

  • Connecting to an outbound outreach service, so introductions can reach people outside the membership.
  • Introductions across communities.

Required profile details

Curated Introductions needs each of these on the member's profile. A member can turn it on with details missing; they're prompted afterwards to fill in whatever is blank.

What members write

The wording of these prompts will be revised later

The survey becomes six prompts. Each one suggests what to include, but the member decides what to say.

Me · personal

Who you are off the course, and what matters to you.

Me · business

Your industry, your role, the size of your company, and what you offer.

Me · golf

Your skill, pace and attitude, when you like to play, and your habits on the course.

Who I'd like to meet · personal

The kind of person you'd enjoy spending four hours with.

Who I'd like to meet · business

The industries, roles or companies you'd like to be introduced to.

Who I'd like to meet · golf

The kind of golfer you'd like to play with, and anything that's a dealbreaker.

Members who already answered the current survey keep their answers as known facts, so nobody has to start over.

How a match is found

  1. AI reads the answers when they're saved. Each answer becomes a private profile: facts about the member, plus what they must have, won't accept or merely prefer. "I'd rather avoid cigars" lowers a match's ranking; "absolutely no cigars" rules it out.
  2. Rules remove conflicts in both directions. A match must suit both people. A member who has turned Curated Introductions off, or who has already played with the other person, is left out as today.
  3. The rest are ranked. Business fit, golf fit and personal fit, plus how recently each person used the app.
  4. Open questions are asked. If a strong match depends on something one member hasn't said, that member is asked first.
  5. An admin decides. The admin sees each suggestion with both members' own words quoted, including gender, habits and preferences, and approves or rejects it.

Rules for these questions

  • Only dealbreakers trigger a question. A soft preference just affects the ranking.
  • Only strong matches trigger a question, so members aren't asked about every candidate.
  • Questions are batched: if a match depends on two answers, one message asks both ("…two quick questions about cigars and music on the course").
  • The same mechanism covers any dealbreaker that can be answered with a few choices, such as music, alcohol or gender.
  • If an answer contradicts what the member later writes, an admin sees the conflict and the most recent statement wins.
  • The choices appear on a screen in the app rather than as buttons on the notification, because iPhones can't show buttons on web push notifications.

After an admin approves

  • Both members get a push and an email saying there's a new match in their Curated Introductions queue. Today only one side gets the match in their list.
  • The queue is under More on a phone, and is its own item in the left navigation on a computer.
  • No reasons are shown. The "Why you two" note, the shared-interest chips and the reason in the notification are removed.
  • Tapping the name opens the member's profile, including their LinkedIn link.
  • From there either member can message the other or invite them to a round. "Not now" still hides the match and keeps the pair apart for 90 days.

Privacy and safety

  • Members' private answers are shown only to admins, never on a profile or to the other member.
  • Only the text members write for Curated Introductions is sent to the AI service. Names, emails and phone numbers are not.
  • The AI's output is checked against a fixed list of categories, so text a member writes can't instruct it to do anything else.
  • Age and gender are private and used only for matching.
  • Nationality, religion and ethnicity are not golf-relevant, so the survey doesn't ask about them and they are never factors in matching. If a member mentions them in their own words, the AI ignores them, and the admin is told so they can follow up if needed.
Before launch: it's worth a lawyer briefly confirming the use of gender and age in matching, given the golf reasons above, since both are protected traits in some contexts.

Cost and next steps

Reading one member's answers costs about 2 cents with Claude Opus 5.5. Answers are only read when they change, so the running cost is small.

  1. Import member data from GoHighLevel into member profiles, so the required details and LinkedIn URLs are filled in wherever GoHighLevel has them. This comes first.
  2. Test the reading. A prototype is ready with 54 invented answers from 12 fictional members. It needs an API key to run, and a run costs about $1. It currently tests industry and role, and would be extended to whole descriptions and dealbreakers.
  3. Ship the simpler member flow. Two-sided matches, no reasons shown, the new queue, the required profile details, and matching across the community. This doesn't depend on AI.
  4. Build open-ended matching once the test results are good, with the dealbreaker questions.
  5. Move existing members across using their current answers.
